Exhibition highlights in October
Vienna´s museums are home to spectacular art exhibitions that are just waiting to be discovered. Let yourself be wowed by these current exhibitions.

Großansicht:
“Crime Scene Ephesus”: The fall exhibition of the Kunsthistorisches Museum Wien explores an ancient mystery involving Cleopatra, Caesar and others through an exciting audio tour. At the Neue Hofburg located on Heldenplatz. (Architectural elements of the Octagon from Ephesus)
&ndash;
© KHM-Museumsverband
Großansicht:
In the compelling exhibition “Picasso - Bacon. What It Feels Like to Be Human", the Albertina presents the two most significant figurative painters of the 20th century, Pablo Picasso and Francis Bacon. (Pablo Picasso, Dora Maar, 1940)
&ndash;
© Succession Picasso / Bildrecht, Wien 2026, © Foto: bpk/Nationalgalerie, SMB, Museum Berggruen/ Jens Ziehe
Großansicht:
“Erika Giovanna Klien. All Is Movement”: A comprehensive solo exhibition at the Unteres Beldedere showcases the full range of the Austrian-American artist’s work, extending beyond her early kinetic pieces. (Exhibition view)
&ndash;
© Johannes Stoll / Belvedere, Wien
Großansicht:
“Elisabeth von Samsonow” at the Heidi Horten Collection shows how the German-Austrian multidisciplinary artist explores ecological issues through colorful sculptures and paintings. Her works engage in a dialogue with pieces from the collection (exhibition view).
&ndash;
© Simon Veres
Großansicht:
The new director of the mumok - Museum of Modern Art, Fatima Hellberg, is showcasing “Terminal Piece,” an exhibition in five acts featuring the eponymous installation by US artist Kate Millett. (exhibition view)
&ndash;
© Foto: Markus Wörgötter
Großansicht:
The dialogue exhibition “Herbert Boeckl / Hans Josephsohn: Figural Archetypes” at the Leopold Museum brings together, for the first time, works by two major artists of modernism - Austrian painter Herbert Boeckl and Swiss sculptor Hans Josephsohn. (Herbert Boeckl, Akt vor gelber Kiste, 1925 und Hans Josephsohn, Ohne Titel, 1969)
&ndash;
© Leopold Museum, Wien
Großansicht:
The MuseumsQuartier is celebrating its 25th anniversary with “Vision and Resistance” at MQ Freiraum. The Second Republic’s largest cultural development project has transformed Vienna and polarised like few others.
&ndash;
© MuseumsQuartier Wien/Thomas Meyer
Großansicht:
“The Great Michelangelo is Dead! Avant-Garde and Academicism”: To mark the 300th anniversary of the founding of the Vienna Academy of Fine Arts, the exhibition brings together historic European masterpieces and contemporary art (exhibition view).
&ndash;
© Kunstsammlungen der Akademie der bildenden Künste Wien, Foto: Iris Ranzinger
Großansicht:
The WestLicht Museum of Photography hosts the annual “World Press Photo" exhibition, the most important presentation of international press photography, for the 25th time. (Chantal Pinzi, Farīsāt: Gunpowder’s Daughters)
&ndash;
© Chantal Pinzi, Panos Pictures
Großansicht:
Until October, 26: At its locations at the MuseumsQuartier and Karlsplatz, the Kunsthalle Wien presents “Lebt und arbeitet in Wien. Contemporary Art from Vienna”, its largest exhibition of contemporary Viennese art in more than a decade. The exhibition features over 130 works by 56 artists living and working in Vienna. (Exhibition view)
&ndash;
© Markus Wörgötter
Großansicht:
“Good Collecting – Bad Collecting”: To mark its 150th anniversary, the Natural History Museum Vienna takes the opportunity to look back - critically, at times - on its own history. (Exhibition view)
&ndash;
© NHM Wien, Chloe Potter
Großansicht:
“A Muslim, a Christian, and a Jew – Eran Shakine” at the Jewish Museum Vienna: In his large-scale works, the Israeli artist humorously explores the similarities and differences between the three world religions. (Eran Shakine, A Muslim, a Christian, and a Jew Turn the Same Wheel)
&ndash;
© Eran Shakine
Großansicht:
“Documents of Injustice. The Case of Freud” at the Sigmund Freud Museum sheds light on the last months of the Freud family in Nazi occupied Vienna and the years that followed. Newly discovered documents complement this special exhibition. (Sigmund and his brother Alexander Freud, photo from Harry Freud's album, Grinzing, 1937)
&ndash;
© Sigmund Freud Museum
Großansicht:
In “The Global Power of Love. A Journey Through the Centuries” the Austrian National Library aims to highlight love as a source of artistic inspiration. Among the approximately 100 exhibits are historical love letters. (Piano score for Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart’s Le Nozze di Figaro, 1809)
&ndash;
© Österreichische Nationalbibliothek
