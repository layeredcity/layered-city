Current and Upcoming Exhibitions at the Gropius Bau
Exhibition
Christoph Schlingensief: Es ist nicht mehr mein Problem, macht eure Scheiße alleine
9 October 2026 to 17 January 2027
Over the course of his artistic career, Christoph Schlingensief (1960–2010) relentlessly confronted the absurdities of the present. The exhibition Es ist nicht mehr mein Problem, macht eure Scheiße alleine (It’s not my problem anymore, deal with your own shit) at Gropius Bau sheds light on his visionary, genre-defying body of work, ranging from striking political actions to projects spanning theatre, opera, film and the visual arts.
To the exhibition ⟨https://www.berlinerfestspiele.de/en/gropius-bau/programm/2026/ausstellungen/christoph-schlingensief⟩
Exhibition
Gabriele Stötzer: Dabei sein und nicht schweigen
19 June to 6 December 2026
For more than five decades, Gabriele Stötzer has been grappling with questions of justice, gender and self-determination. Her own body often plays a central role in her work – not as an object, but as a site of resistance and feminist self-assertion. Dabei sein und nicht schweigen is the artist’s largest institutional solo exhibition to date.
Learn more ⟨https://www.berlinerfestspiele.de/en/gropius-bau/programm/2026/ausstellungen/gabriele-stoetzer⟩
Exhibition
Kreuzberg: Kunst und Migration seit 1960
10   September   2026 to 17   January   2027
Nowhere were the social and cultural changes of 1960s and 1970s West Germany more powerfully manifest than in Berlin’s Kreuzberg neighbourhood.
The group exhibition focuses on the diverse and largely overlooked cultural production on the topic of labour migration and on how artists in the neighbourhood have responded to the tensions and issues of their time.
Learn more ⟨https://www.berlinerfestspiele.de/en/gropius-bau/programm/2026/ausstellungen/kreuzberg⟩
A Play Space for Kids
BAUBAU
Since 14 March 2026
Playing, laughing, making noise, letting off steam, doing nothing – all in an exhibition venue! With BAUBAU, the artist Kerstin Brätsch designed an admission-free play space for kids, where more is allowed than forbidden. On Gropius Bau’s ground floor, colourful wallpapers, structures, objects and open-ended material called “loose parts” configure flexible spaces that are shaped by children’s activities.

Anniversary 75 Years of the Berliner Festspiele In 2026 the Berliner Festspiele celebrate their 75th anniversary. A selection of events programmed throughout the entire year engage take the anniversary as their theme or mark the occasion with works at the Haus der Berliner Festspiele, the Gropius Bau, the Philharmonie, on Berlin’s Ringbahn and at other locations in the city. Outstanding international that liberal societies and their cultural institutions face in a new, multipolar world order. artists such as Marina Abramović, Gabriele Stötzer and Akira Takayama will set the agenda both thematically and aesthetically. Entitled “Die große Unordnung” (The Great Disorder) , the four-day focus Reflexes & Reflections will also enquire about the challenges. Learn more ⟨https://www.berlinerfestspiele.de/en/programm/2026/75-jahre-berliner-festspiele⟩
27 Stations on the Ringbahn What If Berlin As part of the 75th anniversary of the Berlin Festspiele ⟨https://www.berlinerfestspiele.de/en/programm/2026/focus-japan⟩ , Japanese theatre artist Akira Takayama invited audiences with What If Berlin to embark on tours led by 27 guides aboard the Ringbahn. Over four weekends, more than 3,000 visitors took part in over 350 tours covering all 27 Ringbahn stations, exploring alternative visions of Berlin’s future. In the audience vote that followed, he asked participants to decide which of the projects he had presented would be most needed in Berlin’s future, and which of the proposals deserved to be realised. The public vote ultimately named the Central Site of Learning and Remembrance at Treptower Park S-Bahn station as the audience’s favourite project.
