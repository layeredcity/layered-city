Jazzfest Berlin 2026
DREAM BABY DREAM
Trailer Jazzfest Berlin 2026, Design: 3pc, Music: Ready Beat – Ches Smith: Clone Row
© Berliner Festspiele / Jazzfest Berlin
To this day, Berlin remains a refuge, a place of longing and a blank canvas for people from all over the world. This is especially true of the city’s international art and music scene, with its atmosphere of openness, diversity and constant renewal. This urban pluralism is eloquently expressed by the intergenerational short film DREAM BABY DREAM , created in collaboration with 60 Berliners as part of Jazzfest Berlin 2025 and providing its theme for 2026. This award-winning collective film project opens the 63rd festival edition at Haus der Berliner Festspiele and explores questions of belonging, utopia, dreams and trauma through music, movement and poetry.
Jazzfest Berlin has always seen itself as a place where tradition meets the future and local perspectives meet global experiences, hosting encounters between different communities, working methods and forms of expression. This year, Camila Nebbia, Mariá Portugal and ganavya – Berlin-based artists originally from Argentina, Brazil and the United States – embody this dialogue, moving effortlessly between continents, artistic scenes and musical languages. Christian Lillinger’s internationally cast world premiere Open Form For Society II presents a radically condensed form of chamber music, with five musicians exploring the bandleader’s intricate compositions on keyboard instruments. BONECRUSHER, an extraordinary orchestra led by Matthias Muche, brings together an impressive ensemble of 12 trombones at the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church.
For six decades now, Anthony Braxton has explored liminal spaces, expanded aesthetic boundaries and invented new musical systems: “My music is like my life: It’s in between these areas.” Fifty years after his first appearance at the Berliner Jazztage and seven years after his remarkable Sonic Genome performance at Gropius Bau, the pioneering multi-instrumentalist, composer and educator returns to the Jazzfest Berlin with a septet created especially for this occasion: “What we call jazz is a sonic continuum. It’s not just about celebration of the past; it’s about the hope of the future.”
Over four days, the 2026 edition of the festival will bring together 24 acts featuring more than 135 musicians from over 25 countries, including four world premieres, two European premieres and five German premieres. Among the guests are some of the most influential voices in the international creative music scene, including the CHICAGO UNDERGROUND Duo, the Tomeka Reid Quartet, the ensemble Twin Talk, Clone Row led by the versatile drummer Ches Smith, the all-star trio Graewe/Drake/Jones and the visionary saxophonist Immanuel Wilkins’s acclaimed quartet, all from the United States. The human voice also plays a central role in this year’s programme. Artists such as Karoline Wallace, Alejandra Charry, Wendy Eisenberg and Amir ElSaffar move between song, improvisation and transcultural sonic research. Jazzfest Berlin welcomes two outstanding artists from the United Kingdom, Alexander Hawkins and Laura Jurd, as well as key figures of the Scandinavian scene, including Elin Forkelid and the ensemble (Exit) Knarr led by bassist Ingebrigt Håker Flaten. From Japan, Otomo Yoshihide joins the festival with his quintet, while Iranian clarinettist Shabnam Parvaresh presents her Sheen Trio at Jazzfest Berlin for the first time.
Creating spaces for encounters, experimentation and the inclusive, sometimes rebellious spirit of jazz, passing this on to future generations and engaging new audiences are at the heart of this year’s Jazzfest Community Week. As part of a multi-day workshop, 25 students from the Jazz Institute Berlin will immerse themselves in Braxton’s musical universe and join him to develop the JIB Creative Music Orchestra. Once again, the district of Moabit will become a hub for numerous neighbourhood and lunchtime concerts, as well as two workshops that playfully combine music, theatre and circus. For the fourth year in a row, the five-day ImproLabs for Kids will welcome around 30 primary school children to KULTURFABRIK Moabit and Kulturmanege Moabit.
The festival will conclude with a Berlin legend: Alexander von Schlippenbach and his Globe Unity Orchestra. The ensemble made music history in the 1960s when it challenged audiences at the Philharmonie Berlin with its uncompromising free jazz. Sixty years after its Jazzfest Berlin debut, the orchestra returns with Apokalyptische Nabelschau to close a festival edition that brings together more than 200 artists, young people, children and adults from Berlin and around the world, aged between six and eighty-eight. Together they embody the artistic diversity, cosmopolitan spirit and innovative strength that have always defined jazz: Dream Baby Dream!

Jazzfest Community Week
For its community and outreach programme, Jazzfest Berlin is cooperating this year with 15 local initiatives from Moabit and with students from the Jazz Institute Berlin. Eight neighbourhood concerts at six venues, a Creative Music Workshop led by Anthony Braxton, two workshops for children, decolonial city tours, a seminar and film screenings open the festival to local communities and audiences alike, inviting them to take an active part in the programme.

Key Visual, Jazzfest Community Week 2026
Think Globally, Act Locally
Community Building in an Ever-Changing World
© Berliner Festspiele / Jazzfest Berlin, video: Andrés Aguiló
Berlin, as a cultural and artistic metropolis, continues to be a place of aspiration and refuge for people from around the world. But what does it mean to leave one’s home country, friends and family behind in order to immerse oneself in the international music scene of another city? How do musicians who have chosen Berlin as a place to live and work perceive its diverse arts landscape, and how does their perspective on their own country and on a globalised world in transition change? What does all of this mean for their lives as artists?
Six artists who have made Berlin their home, all internationally acclaimed musicians and participants in this year’s Jazzfest Berlin, offer insights into their lives and artistic work in the German capital. At the same time, they reflect on the importance of an open, inclusive, locally rooted and internationally connected community as a counterforce to the radicalising and divisive forces shaping our societies.

Film Screenings at Jazzfest Berlin 2026
The experimental short film DREAM BABY DREAM lends its title to the 63rd edition of Jazzfest Berlin and will be screened several times throughout the festival. The two documentaries Being Hipp and Soundtrack to a Coup d’Etat explore the cultural, historical and political dimensions of jazz. In addition, Jazz on Screen presents seven jazz films over seven days at b-ware! Ladenkino.

Artist Talk with Wadada Leo Smith With contributions by Paola Malavassi and Vijay Iyer As part of his final European tour, legendary trumpeter and composer Wadada Leo Smith was appearing at Jazzfest Berlin 2025. Along with music journalist Peter Margasak and Nadin Deventer, artistic director of Jazzfest Berlin, he looked back on more than half a century of “Creative Music”, talked about his experiences past and present as an American musician in Europe, and reflected on the importance of self-organised musicians’ associations like the AACM for the future.
